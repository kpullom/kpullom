Welcome!

I’m a Ph.D. candidate in Education Policy at the University of Pennsylvania, with a B.S. in Chemistry from MIT. I am also pursuing an A.M. in Statistics and Data Science from the Wharton School.

My research examines how policies and interventions that support families in economic hardship shape children’s educational and economic trajectories. Drawing on quantitative methods and cross-sector administrative data that link social service systems and education, I examine patterns and disparities in access, and how access to broader systems of support connects to human capital development and economic mobility. I also conduct economic evaluations of these policies to measure the costs and consequences of these supports.

In my dissertation work, I use marginal structural models with inverse probability of treatment weighting to examine the relationship between SNAP take-up and the concentration of community resources (i.e., preschools, schools, and churches) in North Carolina. I leverage dynamic shifts in treatment as counties gain or lose institutions. I find that more of these institutions per child is associated with higher take-up, particularly in smaller, higher-poverty counties with fewer resources overall. These findings suggest that connecting families to community institutions, and having agencies partner with them, may improve access and promote child welfare, especially where opportunity is limited.

I am on the 2026 - 2027 job market.

Research

I apply quantitative methods to examine connections between policies and interventions that support families in economic hardship and children’s educational and economic trajectories. My research examines how educational institutions and broader systems of support can shield children from the adverse effects of childhood poverty. I examine the effects of these policies and interventions and how they can efficiently identify, support, and connect families to the resources they need to thrive to improve both access and impact.

These large investments require political will and sustained investment, so I also examine the economic consequences of investments and the cost of interventions to provide policymakers with comprehensive and rigorous evidence of educational and social policy investments.

As a predoctoral fellow with the Institute of Education Sciences, I completed research placements with the Urban Institute and the North Carolina Department of Public Instruction.
